# Facet definitions — 3.1

Use only explicit source evidence. Problem is the limitation/question; task is
the operational objective; method is how it is performed; dataset is a named
data/resource used; contribution is the claimed new artifact/finding.

Separate distinct items within a facet. The same artifact may occupy different
facets when the paper explicitly supports both roles. Do not infer hidden names,
novelty, missing experiments or unsupported conclusions. An evidence quotation
proves grounding, not semantic correctness. Generated labels remain unreviewed
silver and require independent source review for claims of semantic accuracy.

Growing interest, popularity and generic context alone do not establish a problem.
Preserve limitations and conditions on findings, including partial success; do
not keep only the positive half of a qualified claim. Source text corruption and
non-research boilerplate must remain visible in the audit. Empty facets are valid
when unsupported, and are not evidence that the input is a usable research paper.
